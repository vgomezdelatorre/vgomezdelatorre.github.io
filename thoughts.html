  • April 2026 Leadership

    On trust as infrastructure

    I've been thinking about trust less as a soft thing and more as infrastructure. The same way a building needs plumbing and wiring before walls go up, teams need trust before real collaboration can happen. It's load-bearing. When it's there, you don't notice it. When it's missing, everything is slower, louder, and harder than it needs to be.

    Building it is slower than most timelines want to admit. It accrues through small moments of follow-through, honest feedback, and showing up consistently — not through a team offsite or a stated value on a website. You can't install it. You accumulate it.

    The interesting design question: what rituals and rhythms in a team's workflow are actually trust-building exercises in disguise?

  • March 2026 Craft

    The gap between taste and skill

    Ira Glass had the best description of this. Early in a creative career, your taste is already developed — you know what good looks like — but your skill hasn't caught up yet. The gap is uncomfortable. Most people mistake discomfort for a signal to quit rather than a signal that they're on the right path.

    I think about this with designers I work with. The ones who close the gap fastest aren't necessarily the most talented. They're the ones who make more work, seek more feedback, and are willing to feel the discomfort of that distance without making it mean something about them as a person.

    The goal isn't to eliminate the gap — it's to keep your taste running slightly ahead of your execution. That's what keeps the work interesting.

  • February 2026 Process

    Constraints are the design brief

    A constraint isn't a wall. It's a boundary that shapes the space you're designing in. I've noticed that teams who treat constraints as problems to work around often produce weaker solutions than teams who treat them as defining features of the problem.

    When you internalize the constraint — really understand why it exists — you start making decisions from inside the problem rather than pushing against it from the outside. The energy shifts. You stop trying to find the loophole and start finding the opportunity.

    Some of the best work I've seen came directly from a constraint that initially felt impossible. The deadline forced prioritization. The technical limit created a simpler interaction. The budget removed every option except the right one.

    Constraints don't limit creativity. They focus it.
