Project Title Three

A short summary of this project, the problem it solved, and the impact it had on users or the business. One or two sentences that give the reader the shape of the work.

Hero image

Overview

Set the scene here. What was the product, the team, the moment in the company's life? What were you responsible for? Give the reader enough context to understand the stakes without drowning in background.

The challenge

What was the core problem? Be specific about what was broken, unclear, or missing. The best case studies name the tension clearly — what was pulling in opposite directions, and why was it hard?

Include constraints that shaped the work: timeline, resources, organizational dynamics, technical limitations. These aren't excuses — they're the design brief.

Supporting image
Supporting image

Approach

Walk through how you worked. What decisions did you make, and why? This is where your thinking shows — not just what you did, but how you navigated ambiguity, brought others along, and made trade-offs.

If you led a team through this, talk about how you structured the work, how you ran reviews, how you handled disagreement. The process is part of the craft.

Process or outcome image

Outcome

What changed? Be as specific as you can — metrics, qualitative shifts, team or organizational impact. If you can't share numbers, describe what moved: team confidence, decision speed, product clarity.

What would you do differently? A sentence or two of honest reflection shows maturity and makes the rest of the story more credible.
